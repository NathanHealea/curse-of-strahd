# Character Growth

These three events paint a really compelling arc — Evangelina's zealotry is being _tested_ by Barovia in escalating ways. Here are some character development threads you could pull on:

**The Strahd Encounter as a Crack in Her Armor**

She attacked a creature of shadow on pure instinct and faith — and Pelor's light wasn't enough. That's theologically devastating for someone like Evangelina. She could start experiencing doubt she'd never admit aloud: not in Pelor, but in _herself_ as His instrument. Does she pray more fervently now? Does she flinch in moments she never would have before? A near-death experience from the very darkness she's sworn to destroy should leave a mark deeper than the physical scars.

**The Coffin Maker as a Mirror**

This is the big one. She asked him to repent, and when he didn't (or couldn't), she burned him alive. On the surface, this is perfectly consistent with her backstory — "the stubborn and the wicked must be judged and burned." But Barovia should make her sit with that. Consider:

- Does she feel righteous about it, or does something nag at her? A man burning alive on his own coffin is a dark image, even for a zealot.
- Did any party members react with horror? Their discomfort could become a slow-building tension.
- Most importantly — her Mother Superior unleashed divine fire that destroyed _everything_, friend and foe alike. Evangelina is now _using fire the same way_. Is she becoming the blaze that consumes indiscriminately? That parallel could haunt her if someone points it out.

**The Burgomaster Scene as the Real Turning Point**

This is where it gets rich. Dagbron's point — that the Burgomaster worships Pelor under a different name — strikes at the very foundation of her worldview. She nearly got herself killed not by a vampire lord or a shadow army, but by her own inability to recognize an _ally_. Consider exploring:

- The realization that her definition of "heretic" might be too broad. If a man who worships the same god under a different name is her enemy, then she has more enemies than allies — and in Barovia, she can't afford that.
- The humiliation of the dungeon. She's a Dawnborn sister, anointed in sacred flame, and she was beaten by ordinary guards. Pelor didn't intervene. That silence from her god in a moment of suffering-for-His-name could be read two ways: either she wasn't truly serving Him in that moment, or faith doesn't always shield you from consequences.
- The forced apology is _huge_. Evangelina doesn't apologize. The fact that she did — even if it was tactical survival — means she bent. And once someone like her bends once, the question becomes whether she'll snap back harder or start learning flexibility.

**Threads Going Forward**

Here are a few directions you could take her growth:

Evangelina could start distinguishing between _righteous fire_ and _wrathful fire_. She's been treating them as the same thing, but Barovia is showing her the difference. Burning the coffin maker felt like justice — but was it Pelor's will, or her own rage wearing Pelor's face? This is especially potent given her backstory: the shadow army destroyed her home, and now she's destroying people with the same element that consumed the monastery.

Her relationship with Dagbron could become a key axis of growth. He worships the same god but practices his faith differently. He's essentially the Seraphine figure in her current life — mercy beside zeal. If she keeps pushing him away or ignoring his counsel, she risks losing the one person who understands her faith _and_ challenges it.

You might also consider giving her a moment where mercy _works_ where violence wouldn't have. If someone she spares later becomes an ally, or gives her crucial information about the monastery's fall, it would force her to reckon with how many potential answers she's already burned to ash.

The ultimate question Barovia is asking Evangelina: _Can the Dawnfather's light illuminate without incinerating?_ Her answer to that will define who she becomes.

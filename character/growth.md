# Character Growth

These three events paint a really compelling arc — Evangelina's zealotry is being _tested_ by Barovia in escalating ways. Here are some character development threads you could pull on:

**The Strahd Encounter as a Crack in Her Armor**

She attacked a creature of shadow on pure instinct and faith — and Pelor's light wasn't enough. That's theologically devastating for someone like Evangelina. She could start experiencing doubt she'd never admit aloud: not in Pelor, but in _herself_ as His instrument. Does she pray more fervently now? Does she flinch in moments she never would have before? A near-death experience from the very darkness she's sworn to destroy should leave a mark deeper than the physical scars.

**The Coffin Maker as a Mirror**

This is the big one. She asked him to repent, and when he didn't (or couldn't), she burned him alive. On the surface, this is perfectly consistent with her backstory — "the stubborn and the wicked must be judged and burned." But Barovia should make her sit with that. Consider:

- Does she feel righteous about it, or does something nag at her? A man burning alive on his own coffin is a dark image, even for a zealot.
- Did any party members react with horror? Their discomfort could become a slow-building tension.
- Most importantly — her Mother Superior unleashed divine fire that destroyed _everything_, friend and foe alike. Evangelina is now _using fire the same way_. Is she becoming the blaze that consumes indiscriminately? That parallel could haunt her if someone points it out.

**The Burgomaster Scene as the Real Turning Point**

This is where it gets rich. Dagbron's point — that the Burgomaster worships Pelor under a different name — strikes at the very foundation of her worldview. She nearly got herself killed not by a vampire lord or a shadow army, but by her own inability to recognize an _ally_. Consider exploring:

- The realization that her definition of "heretic" might be too broad. If a man who worships the same god under a different name is her enemy, then she has more enemies than allies — and in Barovia, she can't afford that.
- The humiliation of the dungeon. She's a Dawnborn sister, anointed in sacred flame, and she was beaten by ordinary guards. Pelor didn't intervene. That silence from her god in a moment of suffering-for-His-name could be read two ways: either she wasn't truly serving Him in that moment, or faith doesn't always shield you from consequences.
- The forced apology is _huge_. Evangelina doesn't apologize. The fact that she did — even if it was tactical survival — means she bent. And once someone like her bends once, the question becomes whether she'll snap back harder or start learning flexibility.

**Threads Going Forward**

Here are a few directions you could take her growth:

Evangelina could start distinguishing between _righteous fire_ and _wrathful fire_. She's been treating them as the same thing, but Barovia is showing her the difference. Burning the coffin maker felt like justice — but was it Pelor's will, or her own rage wearing Pelor's face? This is especially potent given her backstory: the shadow army destroyed her home, and now she's destroying people with the same element that consumed the monastery.

Her relationship with Dagbron could become a key axis of growth. He worships the same god but practices his faith differently. He's essentially the Seraphine figure in her current life — mercy beside zeal. If she keeps pushing him away or ignoring his counsel, she risks losing the one person who understands her faith _and_ challenges it.

You might also consider giving her a moment where mercy _works_ where violence wouldn't have. If someone she spares later becomes an ally, or gives her crucial information about the monastery's fall, it would force her to reckon with how many potential answers she's already burned to ash.

The ultimate question Barovia is asking Evangelina: _Can the Dawnfather's light illuminate without incinerating?_ Her answer to that will define who she becomes.

## Session 7: The False Dawn (2026-11-07)

Barovia has been asking her a question since the start, and in [[the-false-dawn|The False Dawn]] (S7) she answered it in the worst way she could. She came to believe she was **the embodiment of Pelor**, and she started judging souls. Three were blessed. **One was turned to ash.** Then she turned on [[Victor]], and a boy in his father's house **turned her into a rat**.

**Where the Arc Stands Now**

Every earlier warning in these notes has now come true:

- **The coffin maker was a rehearsal.** He at least refused to repent before she burned him. This time she lit the fire before anyone had a chance to refuse anything. That is no longer judgment. She is the blaze that consumes indiscriminately.
- **The Mother Superior parallel has closed.** Her Mother called down fire that destroyed friend and foe together. Evangelina has now done the same, on purpose, one person at a time, and she called it a blessing.
- **"His instrument" became "Him."** After Straud, the fear was that she would doubt herself as Pelor's instrument. She overcorrected the other way: if she can't be sure she's His tool, she'll *be* Him. That is pride, and Devotion warns against it.

**The Humiliation Is the Point**

She was not stopped by Straud, a shadow army, or a holy rival. She was stopped by **Victor**, the strange young man shouting for food in the Burgomaster's house, who [[tarot-readings|the Donjon card]] says lives in a prison. He shrank the living sun into a rat. That is how Barovia answers false gods: it makes them small. She had believed herself divine, and as a rat she could not pray, preach, or burn anything.

**Bridge Said It Out Loud**

The old notes guessed that a companion's horror would slowly build into tension. Instead, [[Bridge]] said it plainly when Eva woke: *you burned innocents in the name of Pelor.* It came from Bridge, not Dagsbrun, the faithful counterweight she expected to argue with. Someone who isn't speaking for any god at all has accused her, so she can't dismiss it as a sermon.

**The Light Has Faded, but the Oath Holds**

She is **not an oathbreaker**, and that matters. Pelor did not cast her out. He went quiet. That leaves her in her most dangerous place, between grace and damnation, where either path is still possible:

- She can **double down**: decide the dimming is a test of faith, and burn harder to prove herself. That is the road to breaking her oath.
- She can **repent**: admit that the fire she called holy was her grief over the Dawnspire, wearing Pelor's face. That is the road to the light coming back changed — light that shows the way instead of burning.

**"Pelor Is No Longer a Safe Word"**

Whatever this meant at the table, it fits the arc. His name used to settle everything, as an excuse, a battle cry, and a shield. It doesn't anymore. Every time she says "by the Dawnfather's light" from now on, it should cost her something.

**Seraphine's Absence Is Louder Now**

The twin bond was always mercy beside zeal. On the night she burned a soul to ash, there was no mercy beside her. If she thinks of Seraphine now, it shouldn't be with longing. It should be with shame. *What would my sister say?* She can't bear to know the answer, and that is the most honest thing she could feel.

### Character Moments for Upcoming Sessions

- **Meeting Victor at the [[blue-water-inn|Blue Water Inn]]** — she has to sit across the table from the man who stopped her. Does she threaten him, avoid his eyes, or thank him? Thanking him would be a huge step: admitting that the rat stopped a fire she couldn't stop herself.
- **The name of the one who burned** — sooner or later she'll learn who it was: a name, a family, someone left grieving. Give her someone to mourn the way she mourns the Dawnspire. Then she'll understand that she made someone else's night of ruin.
- **Prayers that fall flat** — if the light has faded, let it show. A healing that comes out weak, or a smite that sputters. Each time, she has to choose whether to blame Barovia or look at herself.
- **A confession to Bridge** — not to Dagsbrun, and not to a priest. Bridge accused her, so Bridge is the one she owes an answer. A paladin of Devotion is held to honesty, so she can't deflect: *"You were right. I don't know how to carry it."*
- **Mercy toward someone who deserves the fire** — the real test isn't sparing the innocent. It's sparing someone guilty. If she lets the [[The Hag|Hag]] or someone like her live — with judgment, but no burning — she'll have learned the lesson Barovia has been teaching her.
- **The Sun Sword** — the party has heard it's a big name in Barovia ([[sunblade|Sunblade]]). If she starts to wonder whether her blade is a legend, it tests her new humility. Would she grab that importance to fill the place where her certainty used to be?
- **[[argynvostholt|Argynvostholt]] and the remembering dead** — *"The dead sometimes remember what the living have forgotten."* A fallen order of knights might be the mirror she needs now: holy warriors who maybe lost their way too, or held their oaths past death. Either way they show her what's ahead.
- **A journal entry she can't send** — a letter to the Dawnfather she starts and can't finish, because for the first time she doesn't know what He would say back (`/journal`).

**The Question Has Changed**

The question used to be *Can the Dawnfather's light illuminate without incinerating?* Now that she has incinerated, it's: ***Can a fire that has burned the innocent be rekindled as light?*** Her answer will decide whether she leaves Barovia redeemed, or as the very darkness that took her home.

### Session 7, Revisited: Threads Still Unpulled (2026-11-24)

The section above covers the fire. A few parts of Session 7 still need a closer look:

**She Raised Her Hand Against the House That Freed Her**

[[Vargis]] is the man who let her out of [[Issac]]'s dungeon ([[the-arrest-of-evangelina|The Arrest]]), and he is the Burgomaster she grudgingly accepted as a fellow servant of the Dawnfather under another name. [[Victor]] is **his son**. On the night she believed herself divine, the first "unclean" soul she went after belonged to the family that had shown her mercy. She repaid grace with judgment. A Devotion paladin owes a debt of gratitude, and she doesn't yet know she has broken it. When Vargis hears about this, how he reacts will matter more to her than anything Straud says.

**The Three Who Were Blessed**

Everyone will ask who was turned to ash. Fewer will ask what became of the **three who were blessed**. They walked through her fire and lived, so they may believe she *is* Pelor made flesh. If they come looking for her, wanting more blessings, wanting to follow her, she will be facing the first members of a cult built around her own false dawn. Rejecting their worship would cost her the last comforting story she has. Accepting it would put her on the road to oathbreaking.

**Fire Without a Name Behind It**

Her Mother Superior burned the Dawnspire **in Pelor's name, with Pelor's blessing**, and Evangelina has always called that a holy sacrifice. Now Pelor has gone quiet over her own fire. Sooner or later she will have to ask the unbearable question in the other direction: *was the blaze that took my home truly His, either?* She doesn't have to answer it. Asking it at all would mark the moment her grief stops being a weapon and becomes a wound she can examine.

**The Rat Remembers**

She was small and voiceless, and nobody heard her, which is how the common folk of Barovia have lived under Straud all along. A paladin who has been powerless for even an hour may find she can **see** the powerless differently. Let her catch herself hesitating before she judges someone who has no way to answer her.

### Additional Moments to Play

- **Facing Vargis** — before Victor, or instead of him. *"Your son stopped me from doing something I cannot undo. I owe him, and you, an apology I don't yet know how to say."*
- **A blessed one finds her** — one of the three thanks her, kneels, and calls her Lady Dawn. Her answer is the test.
- **A spell slot she doesn't spend** — the first fight where she could open with Divine Smite and chooses to talk, shield, or heal instead. She does it quietly, without telling anyone it was a choice.
- **Writing to Seraphine, not Pelor** — if she can't write to the Dawnfather, a letter to her twin might be the only honest prayer she has left (`/journal`).

**Recommended next step:** record who was turned to ash once it is known. Every one of these threads depends on that name.
